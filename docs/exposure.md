# Exposure, Koshi floods 27 September 2024

Generated by `python scripts/priority_nepal.py run` (method `majority`, flood threshold 0.5).

- Flooded area inside the analysed local levels: **129.5 km^2**
- Exposed people (our estimate, **upper estimate**): **15,146**
- UNOSAT estimate, same date: **17,317** (87% of it). Analysis: UNOSAT

**Upper estimate.** The flood map is (a), all water minus JRC permanent water (`nepal/level1/S1_20240927_model_mask.tif`). It also counts the normal Koshi channel and seasonal water (river bars, ponds wet every monsoon), so flooded area, exposed people and the priority list are an upper estimate. This is deliberate: for rescue, under-counting flooded people is worse than over-counting. The new-water map (b) was rejected because it missed most of UNOSAT's flood (`docs/nepal_level1.md`).

## Why the numbers differ

- **Population layer.** We use WorldPop 100 m constrained (people placed only in mapped buildings); UNOSAT used WorldPop unconstrained UN-adjusted 2020 (100 m), which also puts people in unbuilt cells.
- **Flood extent.** Our map is the U-Net ensemble without permanent water, but with the normal channel and seasonal water; UNOSAT draws its extent from its own Sentinel-1 analysis of the same date, against its own 11 June 2024 reference water.
- **Counting rule.** A 100 m cell counts as flooded only when at least half of it is flooded (`majority`); `fraction` counts partial cells proportionally.
- **Area.** Only local levels inside `analysis_bbox` are counted; UNOSAT's figure covers all of its Koshi and Madhesh analysis area.
- **Radar limits.** Flooded settlements (buildings, trees) are often missed by C-band radar, which lowers our count where people actually live.
